Half-Life turns pharmacokinetics into a one-button arcade game. A drug's blood level scrolls across the screen as a live curve. Tap to give a dose. Keep the line in the green therapeutic window: too high and the patient turns toxic, too low and the drug stops working. Dr. Jeff Bullock, PharmD, built it so the hardest ideas in pharmacy school can be learned by feel.

## How It Plays

- Every tap is a dose. Oral doses absorb over time, so the level keeps climbing after you tap. IV doses land instantly
- A dashed forecast line shows where the level goes if you wait. Time a dose just before it leaves the window for a Perfect bonus
- Stay in the window to build a streak multiplier. Drift out and the patient's health bar drops
- A coaching tip at the end of every shift names the mistake that cost you the most

## Five Drugs, Real Curveballs

Acetaminophen teaches the rhythm. Theophylline stacks as the patient quits smoking. IV vancomycin changes as the kidneys fail and recover. Lithium takes an NSAID and a heat wave. The boss is digoxin, with amiodarone and low potassium, where days pass in seconds. Beat it to unlock Night Shift, an endless mode with random interactions that speeds up after every curveball.

The drug ranges and interactions are real and simplified for play. It is a teaching game, not dosing guidance.
