Transcript Alchemy is a non-fiction field guide, not a tour of AI possibility but an execution manual. Its thesis: a transcript of any conversation, once captured, is a database you can question with the right prompt, and doing this consistently builds "Conversational Capital," a compounding asset competitors cannot copy because they were not in the room. The book opens with the origin of its own title, a line Bullock said in a meeting and only kept because the meeting was being transcribed.

Part 0 covers how to capture a transcript from any situation in about sixty seconds, plus a plain-English rundown of consent and recording law. Part 0.5 teaches the core skill: a five-part prompt structure (Role, Source, Task, Format, Fence) and the "Stress Test," a grade-me-out-of-ten prompt Bullock ran on his own meetings and got a six. Part 0.6 is an honest limits chapter on AI fabrication, archive privacy, and where human judgment must stay human.

Parts I through IV apply the same skill to sales and marketing, content, coaching, leadership, self-coaching, relationships, parenting, creative writing, strategy, and finally legacy work: turning a grandparent's stories into an autobiography, building a family historian bot, preserving a loved one's voice, and recording a time-capsule letter. The closing chapter names the underlying loop, "Speak, Analyze, Act, Repeat," and "The Vault" catalogs all 101 plays with prompts, 2026 tool stacks, and an industry index.

## Themes

- Talk as an underused, perishable asset
- Memory as faulty and self-flattering versus the transcript as an honest record
- One prompting skill applied across work, self, craft, and family
- Guardrails against AI fabrication (the Fence: quote real lines, never invent)
- Consent and privacy as non-negotiable before recording others
- Compounding advantage from a captured archive (Conversational Capital)
- Taste, judgment, and accountability as the human's remaining job

## Key Ideas

- Conversational Capital: the compounding, un-copyable asset built by consistently capturing and mining your own conversations
- The Method (Role, Source, Task, Format, Fence): the five-part prompt structure underneath every play in the book
- The Fence: the standing instruction to use only what is in the transcript, quote real lines, and flag rather than invent gaps
- The Stress Test: the 'grade me out of ten' prompt Bullock calls the gateway play, and the one that gave him a six on his own meetings
- Anonymizing to Person A / Person B: stripping names before analyzing tense or relationship conversations to get a neutral read
- Speak, Analyze, Act, Repeat: the closing loop that frames all 101 plays
- The Legacy plays: grandparent autobiographies, a family historian bot, preserving a loved one's voice, and time-capsule letters, run with explicit consent rules

## Who It's For

For founders, salespeople, coaches, consultants, and parents who already record their calls and conversations and never go back to read them. The strongest angle: this is not another book selling the idea that AI is powerful, it hands over 101 copy-paste prompts with a built-in honesty guardrail, so you can run one on a real transcript this afternoon and see a result by tonight.
