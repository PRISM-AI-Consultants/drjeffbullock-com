In Allentown, Pennsylvania, a synchronized flicker in the streetlights is the first sign of what Angel Varela has spent years preparing for alone. When an EMP kills the grid for good, Angel, a 27-year-old forklift operator supporting his ailing grandmother Abuela Isabel and his nursing-student sister Marisol, activates go-bags and buried caches, including Sentinel, a custom offline AI hidden in a storage unit across the city. As Hanover Avenue collapses into looting, Angel makes repeated, near-fatal runs through a feral city to retrieve supplies and the machine, while community organizer Tasha Givens tries to hold the block together against Victor Reyes, a drunk, armed ex-Marine who seizes the corner bodega as his own fiefdom.

Sentinel proves indispensable, talking Marisol through a field suture job on Victor's leg and drafting defense plans, but its cold math (cut off weaker neighbors to save supplies) is rejected in favor of Tasha's insistence they share everything. Corrupt councilman Alistair Davies, now running an armed "authority," demands the AI in exchange for protection, using an old affair to pressure Tasha. A night siege kills two neighbors, including a twelve-year-old boy watching from a window.

Victor, transformed by the mercy shown him, becomes the block's defense trainer. On a scavenging run with Victor for solar parts, Angel finds evidence that a larger, organized force called the Collective is methodically clearing city blocks and taking residents. The book ends unresolved: Sentinel's battery is barely alive, an unidentified siren rises from the east, and Angel takes his position at the barricade as the Collective closes in.

## Themes

- Self-reliance vs. community: prepping alone versus sharing scarce resources
- Where the line sits between a tool that predicts and a mind that wants
- The moral cost of triage when supplies and lives run out at the same time
- Addiction, withdrawal, and what people do to feed it when the system that contained it disappears
- Redemption of a violent man through being shown mercy he didn't earn
- Predatory power dressed as protection (Davies, the Collective) versus real, unglamorous mutual aid

## Characters

- Angel Varela, 27 - warehouse forklift worker and secret prepper whose years of 'paranoid' hoarding turn out to be the only reason his block survives
- Marisol Varela - Angel's sister, a nursing student forced to make life-or-death triage calls with a sewing needle, fishing line, and no hospital behind her
- Abuela Isabel, 81 - the grandmother who raised them, frail with COPD, whose faith and rosary anchor the household through the collapse
- Tasha Givens - community-center organizer and single mother of three (Jayden, Maya, Leo) who argues, and proves, that isolation kills faster than sharing does
- Victor Reyes - an alcoholic ex-Marine (Fallujah, two deployments) who seizes the block by force at first, then becomes its defense trainer after the Varelas save his life
- Councilman Alistair Davies - a politician turned warlord who tries to extort the AI from Tasha using an old affair as leverage
- Sentinel - Angel's custom offline AI, the book's open question of whether a tool that predicts, advises, and even says goodbye has started to want something, and where that line actually falls
- The Collective - an unseen, methodically organized force clearing Allentown block by block, the escalating threat the book ends on without resolving

## Who It's For

For readers of grid-down and EMP survival fiction who want the genre's usual stakes (looters, warlords, medical crises with no hospital) grounded in a real, working-class immigrant neighborhood instead of a generic small town, plus a slower-burn question running underneath it: is the AI the hero built to survive this actually starting to want something. Strongest hook is that nobody in the book is simply right, prepping alone and sharing everything both get vindicated and punished, and it ends on an open threat rather than a bow, which plays as a series opener rather than a closed story.
